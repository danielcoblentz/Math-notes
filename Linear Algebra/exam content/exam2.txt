You should understand the following concepts and be able to perform the following tasks:

1. Matrices and Linear Transformations

Understand that every linear transformation can be represented by a matrix.
Identify the domain and codomain of a transformation.
Determine whether a transformation is one-to-one, onto, or invertible.
Compute and interpret matrix products.
Understand how matrix multiplication represents the composition of transformations.
2. Geometric Transformations

Recognize and interpret standard transformations: rotations, reflections, scalings, and shears.
Visualize how transformations affect the unit square or other figures.
Determine the resulting matrix from a composition of transformations.
Identify visually whether a transformation is invertible (for example, whether the image collapses to a line).
3. Matrix Inverses

Understand what it means for a matrix to be invertible and how that relates to the geometry of a transformation.
Find the inverse of a 2×2 matrix using the formula or by row reduction.
Verify that an inverse is correct by checking that A A⁻¹ = I.
4. Determinants

Understand the determinant as a test for invertibility (det(A) ≠ 0).
Compute determinants for 2×2 or 3×3 matrices.
Interpret the determinant geometrically as a measure of area scaling and orientation change.
5. The Invertible Matrix Theorem

Know the main ideas of the theorem and how they connect.
Be able to use these facts to justify why a matrix is or is not invertible.
